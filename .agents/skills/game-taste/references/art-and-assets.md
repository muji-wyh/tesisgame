# Art direction and asset selection

Read this when calibrating art direction, selecting references, or choosing,
sourcing, replacing, or integrating visual/audio assets.

## Choose a visual system

Translate vague goals such as "premium" into observable choices: dominant shapes,
proportions, value hierarchy, palette relationships, surface treatment, perspective,
light direction, type scale, and motion character. Use only the criteria relevant
to the game. A painted storybook, an abstract puzzle, and a tactile toy world can
all be excellent; combining them unintentionally usually weakens the result.

## Calibrate with inspected anchors

Use a compact comparison set, not a catalog of admired games. Prefer references
with a relevant audience, display scale, and interaction context. A specific
screen, moment, or strong existing asset can anchor one property; no reference
has to be a universal quality standard.

For each selected anchor, retain enough evidence to repeat the comparison:
source/location and relevant frame or moment; what was actually observed; the
principle to transfer; and what does not fit this game. Label concept art,
promotional renders, gameplay captures, and inspected live gameplay accurately.
A cinematic does not establish in-game readability or interaction feel.

Use explicit user feedback to refine the existing project reference record or
the task's working criteria: retain which property was accepted or rejected and
why. Approval of one property is not approval of the whole reference. Keep
proposed anchors distinct from user-approved ones; silence is not approval.

Use a useful contrast when the choice is unclear: another viable treatment, an
earlier revision, or a specific conflicting property. Compare fair alternatives,
not a polished favorite against an intentionally weak straw man. Name the real
trade-off, such as ornament versus legibility or weight versus repeated-action
speed, without treating either side as universally superior.

Translate the selected properties into a few observable acceptance criteria.
For example, "the reward silhouette remains readable at the target phone size
while support effects stay subordinate" is checkable; "make it AAA" is not.
Explain what will deliberately not carry over, such as a reference's threatening
tone or cinematic delay. Transfer the principle, not the whole style or layout.

Stop collecting references once the decision has usable anchors. If none can be
inspected, state the gap and use project constraints and the observed baseline
for provisional recommendations. Do not turn uninspected links, a famous title,
or personal preference into a supposedly curated benchmark or completed review.

## Inspect before integration

Check the asset at the intended size and background. For a character or prop,
inspect silhouette, face, edge quality, material detail, and animation extremes.
Check scale, pivot, frame padding, occlusion, and perspective against the scene.
For audio, inspect the attack, decay, noise floor, clipping, and tonal fit through
playback when available; waveform inspection alone does not establish timbre.

Do not stretch a mismatched asset to simulate correct perspective or rely on glow
to conceal a poor silhouette. Check available layers, rig, frames, and transparent
backgrounds before promising a motion design. A static image is not an animated
character; list rigging, repainting, or segmentation as actual remaining work.

For child-facing creatures, inspect the face and the most intense action poses.
Convey majesty through proportion, composition, ornament, scale cues, and gentle
motion. Follow project restrictions on frightening expressions, exposed teeth,
skull imagery, or threat. Do not impose this tone on unrelated adult genres.

## Track rights and real status

Reuse the project's provenance format. For each newly selected asset, retain:

| Field | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Source and creator | Original page or supplied source, author/studio |
| License | Exact terms or license file, attribution obligations, relevant use limits |
| Status | Preview only, acquired locally, converted, or integrated and verified |
| Local material | Actual file location and variant/version where applicable |
| Animation | Included clips/layers and what was inspected; missing work stated explicitly |

"Free download" does not establish commercial or redistribution rights. Record
unknown terms as unknown. Do not ship uncertain source material as licensed or
describe an unpurchased marketplace preview as an acquired asset. If acquisition
needs payment or authorization, identify the exact candidate and pending action
while proceeding with work that does not depend on it. Do not ask for permission
again when the user already supplied the necessary authorization.

Generated artwork is not a shortcut around the project's source requirements.
Use it only when allowed by the requested art direction and task; record its
origin honestly. Basic UI containers and layout geometry do not need to become
external artwork when they already fit the approved style.

## Integrate coherently

Match a small representative set before expanding: subject, environment, interface
accent, and key sound as needed. Preserve the source silhouette and detail while
adapting pivots, crop, palette, scale, and mix. Keep variants visibly intentional
rather than unrelated asset packs in the same scene.

Verify the processed files, not just the source previews: texture edges and
compression, animation framing, material/lighting response, audio attack and tail,
memory/loading behavior, and license attribution. Keep unacquired art selection
separate from the game's active assets.
