# Legal & Paralegal expert review brief

This original communication-learning draft is intended for teenagers and adults. It requires qualified English-language and local legal-domain review before release. The content teaches recognition, workplace discussion, and accurate handling of uncertainty. It does not approve legal advice, filing methods, deadlines, time calculations, case strategy, qualification equivalence, disclosure permissions, conflict exceptions, contract wording, legal validity, eligibility, or case outcomes.

The candidate contains 400 specialist sense memberships and 80 expressions across eight modules, plus unchanged Foundation for 500 distinct senses and 100 expressions. There are 254 new and 146 reused specialist senses. Each specialist module contains exactly 50 senses and 10 expressions. Fifteen sources were actually consulted, with explicitly bounded scope. Every specialist sense has an actual scored route within its own module.

New morphology contains 202 pending graphs: 196 composite candidates and six atomic candidates. Fifty-two new analyses intentionally remain null. Atomic and null senses have same-module Phrase focus. Fourteen corrected scratch tests pass; structural validation, exact spelling reconstruction, canonical reuse, and coverage are checked. These results are not qualified linguistic or legal approval.

All language, domain, morphology, regional usage, pronunciation, and image-suitability gates remain pending. Picture suitability is false. No images, source assets, or recordings have been acquired. Each new sense has a pending artwork brief. Future artwork must satisfy the repository art direction and record the original source, creator, license, acquisition status, and available animations. Exact Ava voice settings are recorded without claiming generated or reviewed audio.

## Scope for reviewers

| Professional boundaries | Check lawyer, paralegal, legal secretary, caseworker, partner, associate, and in-house counsel without implying universal duties, qualifications, or permission. |
| Regional legal titles | Solicitor, barrister, and trainee solicitor retain England and Wales context. Public defender retains U.S. context. Are all relevant local differences clear? |
| Paralegal alternative | Review the proposed U.S. occupational use of legal assistant as an alternative for paralegal. Do not merge legal secretary into that role. |
| Court and institution terms | Review court, tribunal, registry, courtroom, courthouse, and chambers. Institutional structures and court personnel vary. |
| Legal aid | Confirm the distinction between assistance through funding arrangements and a universal entitlement. No eligibility test is supplied. |
| Party labels | Review claimant and the jurisdiction-qualified plaintiff alternative, respondent, appellant, litigant, and opposing party. Do not imply uniform procedural positions. |
| Case and client meanings | Confirm reuse of case file, office, contact, and administrative terms. Prospect reuses the potential-customer sense in a legal-services context; no special professional-duty status is inferred. |
| Court records | Docket is explicitly U.S. Court-record access varies. A record, copy, or source label does not establish completeness, authenticity, or permission to access. |
| Complaint and defense | Complaint has the selected U.S. initiating-document sense. Defense/defence has the selected England and Wales civil-response context and spelling caveat. |
| Document identity | Keep original document, draft version, copy, official copy, certified copy, and sealed record distinct. Issuance and attestation are not interchangeable. |
| Affidavit and statement | Review affidavit, witness statement, factual statement, and statement of truth without prescribing wording or claiming a statement has been verified. |
| Exhibits and schedules | Check exhibit, annex-related reuse, schedule, clause, and page/document references in the intended regional contexts. |
| Execution and signature | Review execution, signatory, counterpart, deed, and attestation-related vocabulary. No signing method or validity determination is taught. |
| Headnote | Keep the editorial summary separate from the judgment and from binding authority. The original draft does not offer a shortcut for adequate legal research. |
| Primary and secondary sources | Preserve the legal-research distinction between authoritative legal material and explanatory commentary without promising that every primary source is binding. |
| Legislation | Bill is proposed legislation; legislation, statute, primary legislation, secondary/delegated legislation, and statutory instrument retain their distinct scope. |
| UK legislative language | Statutory instrument remains UK-specific. No forecast of enactment or instructions for legislative procedure are included. |
| Case law and common law | Case law includes interpretation of statutes; the selected common-law sense concerns law developed through decisions. Avoid treating these as identical in every context. |
| Precedent | Review binding versus persuasive authority, jurisdiction, court hierarchy, ratio decidendi, and obiter dictum. A citation alone does not establish applicability. |
| Research workflow language | Research scope, search history, citation, pinpoint reference, and source currency remain language concepts, not a checklist for sufficient legal research. |
| Legislative status | Repeal, amendment, commencement, and in-force concepts need local review. A consolidated text or date alone does not resolve applicability. |
| Legal interpretation | Verify construction, interpretation, provision, and section definitions without suggesting mechanically certain meaning. |
| Civil proceedings | Review proceedings, claim, defense, counterclaim, joinder, hearing, trial, appeal, order, and judgment against local terminology. |
| Formal application | Application is a formal legal-process request, distinct from software. Motion is contextual and is not accepted automatically as a global synonym. |
| Service and filing | Service and filing are different acts under applicable requirements. A filing receipt does not decide acceptance or compliance. No method or deadline is supplied. |
| Settlement and consent order | Review settlement, consent order, mediation, arbitration, and alternative dispute resolution without implying that all have the same effect or enforceability. |
| Stay and discontinuance | Check procedural meanings and regional limits. The examples discuss status or uncertainty without directing legal action. |
| Remedies | Review damages, remedy, injunction, and costs. No award, entitlement, enforcement, or outcome is promised. |
| Without prejudice | England and Wales civil context requires careful local review. A label alone does not establish protection. No reusable heading or permission rule is taught. |
| Agreements and contracts | Agreement need not be an enforceable contract. Offer, acceptance, consideration, obligation, term, breach, and remedy must not imply universally sufficient legal elements. |
| Contract provisions | Contract term is express or implied content defining rights or obligations; clause is a textual unit; provision is an operative rule or requirement. Entire agreement, severability, indemnity, force majeure, and covenant describe concepts. No operative contract clauses are provided for reuse. |
| Intellectual property | Review intellectual property, copyright, trademark, license/licence, assignment, and permitted-use concepts. No entitlement or legal clearance is implied. |
| Agency and parties | Agency, principal, agent, party, and signatory require context. A role label does not by itself prove authority. |
| Property | Review ownership, title, property, tenant, landlord, lease, easement, and conveyancing language across intended jurisdictions. |
| Privacy versus confidentiality | Privacy, confidentiality, and legal privilege are separate concepts. Existing shared definitions remain canonical but require domain review in this course. |
| Legal professional privilege | England and Wales legal advice privilege and litigation privilege require jurisdiction-specific review. U.S. attorney-client privilege remains explicitly regional. |
| Labels and privilege | A confidentiality label, disclosure log, privileged-document label, or communication route must not be portrayed as creating privilege or disclosure authority. |
| Conflicts and barriers | Review own-interest conflict, client conflict, prospective client, information barrier, and conflict check. A check or barrier is not automatically permission to act. |
| Confidentiality undertaking | Review wording as a concept without providing a reusable undertaking, promising enforcement, or resolving permitted disclosure. |
| Withdrawal of consent | The sole shared definition amendment broadens intervention to action or purpose, preserving the clinical example and all existing metadata. Effects remain context dependent, and prior use is not automatically erased. |
| Information handling | Access permissions, redaction, retention, audit trail, and secure records require local legal and organizational review. No universal retention duration is supplied. |
| Evidence | Evidence, fact, allegation, inference, authenticity, provenance, relevance, and admissibility must remain distinct. Credibility concerns an account or testimony, while source reliability concerns dependability for research. Circumstantial evidence supports a fact through inference; no weight or truth is predetermined. |
| Hearsay | The current draft follows the consulted U.S. federal definition: a statement outside its maker's testimony at the current trial or hearing, offered for its asserted truth, subject to applicable definitions and exclusions. Do not generalize it across jurisdictions or teach admissibility decisions. |
| Leading question | The question's wording points toward an answer. No permitted use, tactical technique, or universal examination rule is taught. |
| Burden and standard | Burden of proof and standard of proof retain defined-proceeding context. No universal threshold or proof method is presented. |
| Witness communication | Witness, testimony, examination-in-chief, cross-examination, and re-examination require regional review. No preparation strategy or prescribed answer is taught. |
| Oath and affirmation | The selected oath sense is religious; affirmation is nonreligious. Check local language and equal respect; no prescribed words are supplied. |
| Deposition and subpoena | These entries retain U.S. procedural context. They teach recognition without instructions for attendance, response, service, or noncompliance. |
| Summons | Do not equate its role, force, or response requirements across jurisdictions. Its morphology remains null, not summon plus plural s. |
| Office coordination | Confirm existing record, task, scheduling, correspondence, supervision, and billing senses fit legal office communication without inventing authority. |
| Homographs | New legal meanings of respondent, complaint, bill, section, service, hearing, application, and disclosure must remain separate from existing nonlegal meanings. |
| Variant handling | Review defense/defence, judgment/judgement, license/licence, claimant/plaintiff, legal assistant, NDA, ADR, legal memorandum/memo, and secondary/delegated legislation in their stated context. Alternatives do not create extra memberships. |
| Morphological candidates | Confirm only real linguistic analyses. Judgment, discontinuance, admissibility, credibility, and severability retain explicit orthographic transformations. Counterpart is counter- prefix plus part, with the corresponding/duplicate sense rather than the physical counter noun. All graphs remain pending. |
| Atomic and null analyses | Court, judge, jury, stay, deed, and oath are six pending atomic candidates. Latin expressions and uncertain lexicalized words remain null and use Phrase practice. |
| Phrase and punctuation structure | Verify the explicitly listed phrase boundaries and hyphens in non-disclosure agreement, examination-in-chief, cross-examination, re-examination, in-house counsel, and attorney-client privilege. Do not infer segmentation from whitespace or length. |
| Artwork and accessibility | Concrete places, documents, and roles need sourced professional artwork; abstract rights and processes may be unsuitable for picture guessing. Do not use stereotypes, authoritative seals, or fictional readable legal forms as evidence of validity. |
| Pronunciation | Review stress, regional variants, Latin terms, acronyms, homographs, and professional titles before audio release. Approved Ava settings alone do not establish pronunciation accuracy. |

## Consulted sources and limits

1. [BLS Paralegals and Legal Assistants](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/paralegals-and-legal-assistants.htm): summary, duties, and work context; U.S. occupational roles only. The page displays an August 27, 2026 update. No admission, filing, or qualification equivalence is inferred.
2. [BLS Lawyers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/legal/lawyers.htm): duties and roles, including in-house counsel, prosecutors, public defenders, associates, and partners. U.S. scope; no authorization for support staff to perform lawyer work.
3. [U.S. Courts glossary](https://www.uscourts.gov/glossary): selected actual passages for affidavit, admissible, appeal/appellant, arbitration, file, jurisdiction, statute, subpoena, testimony, and transcript. Federal context; not exhaustive vocabulary review.

Updated June 30, 2022; SRA-regulated England and Wales practice. The source excludes specific data-protection rights, processing bases, and retention advice. Linked Codes and cases were not read.
5. [U.S. Courts records overview](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-records): case files, dockets, and PACER access versus CM/ECF filing. Linked registration, filing, access rules, fees, retention schedules, and forms were not read.
6. [UK Parliament lawmaking overview](https://www.parliament.uk/about/how/laws/): bill, Act, legislature, primary/secondary legislation, and statutory instruments. Linked detailed bill stages, procedures, and legislation were not read.
7. [SRA conflicts guidance](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/conflicts-interest/): own-interest/client conflicts, prospective clients, and information-barrier context. Updated March 2, 2020; England and Wales scope. No exception or consent condition is taught as authorization.
