# Customer Support expert-review brief

## Scope and status

The Customer Support course contains a complete **draft inventory of 500 distinct canonical senses and 100 expressions**. The two shared foundation modules contribute 100 senses and 20 expressions. Modules03 through module10 add 400 specialist memberships and 80 original expressions: 148 new senses and 252 reused canonical senses. All modules remain draft. Every specialist sense and expression requires recorded domain review in addition to language review, including content outside the dedicated safety module. A `not_required` label cannot bypass this gate.

This course practices frontline issue communication, records, service coordination, and explanation. It does not teach technical repair procedures, credential collection, identity-check bypasses, financial or legal entitlements, retention deadlines, or emergency techniques. Response commitments and account actions depend on current approved arrangements. A workflow example is not permission to change an actual account or system.

All 148 new word images remain sense-specific briefs with picture suitability false. All new pronunciation and expression recordings remain pending with the approved Ava preset. There are 130 explicit pending morphology graphs and 18 deliberately unauthored analyses. Each new sense has an authored scored route through an expression that assesses it or a multi-part graph, but those routes still require content, media, and release review. Source consultation and structural validation are not expert approval.

## Canonical reuse and inventory decisions

The 252 reused senses keep their existing IDs and original on-demand source files. Callback means a return telephone call, not a programming handler. The course reuses both the verb record and the stored-information noun where appropriate, with distinct IDs. Assigning work and a brief pause are genuine new senses; resolved, closed, and reopened are not added as extra counted inflections. Resolution summary, closure reason, and follow-up ticket provide distinct useful concepts instead.

Contact centre, apologise, acknowledgement, and data minimisation are accepted forms, not extra inventory entries. CRM, FCR, and CES are accepted abbreviations of their full terms. Regional alternatives still need review for natural use in the intended course context.

The 18 unauthored analyses are subscription, acknowledge, apologize, clarify, paraphrase, respond, investigate, assign, escalate, forward, resume, redact, expectation, inconvenience, frustration, patience, courtesy, and summary. They remain explicit review work rather than receiving invented syllable cuts. Some intact constituents in the 130 candidate graphs may also require deeper analysis.

## Source and sign-off records

Eight sources were consulted on 2026-10-16 and registered in `sources.json`. BLS and O*NET provide occupational context, not curriculum-frequency rankings or approval of each definition. O*NET excludes occupations primarily devoted to technical support, repair, installation, or sales from this customer-service classification. Zendesk's statuses, transitions, timers, and metric definitions are product-specific. The ICO page states that it is under review and may change; it is not current jurisdiction-wide legal sign-off. The Ombudsman overview was consulted, but its linked detailed chapters were not. Its public-service context is not a universal commercial complaint process. W3C guidance does not certify the accessibility of this game or a service system.

All references remain `pending_term_check`. No reference assets were acquired or copied. Failed retrievals are not registered as consulted pages. Each sign-off must record the canonical or expression ID, reviewer role, launch jurisdiction, source edition and date, exact decision, revised wording where needed, reviewer identity, and review date. Language, domain meaning, morphology, pronunciation, visual suitability, provenance, and delivery are independent gates. A complete inventory stays draft until those gates have evidence.
