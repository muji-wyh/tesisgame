# Clinical Medicine expert review brief

This is an original communication-learning draft for teenagers and adults, not clinical training, diagnostic support or treatment advice. It requires qualified clinical and English-language review before release. No clinical action, examination technique, medicine dose, diagnostic threshold, legal consent rule or professional qualification equivalence is approved by this inventory.

The candidate contains 400 specialist sense memberships and 80 expressions across eight modules, plus unchanged shared Foundation for 500/100 total. There are 153 new and 247 reused specialist senses. Nine sources were actually consulted. New morphology contains 97 pending graphs: 92 composite and five atomic analyses. Fifty-six new analyses remain null. All 400 specialist senses have a scored route within their own module; atomic or null entries are explicitly focused in a same-module Phrase expression.

Thirteen focused scratch tests pass. Structure, exact spelling reconstruction and coverage are verified, but they are not evidence of qualified clinical or linguistic approval. All new review and media statuses remain pending. Picture suitability is false. No images or recordings have been acquired. Approved Ava pronunciation settings are recorded exactly, without generated audio.

## Scope for reviewers

| Area | Specific review questions |
| --- | --- |
| Audience and difficulty | Are the B1/B2 candidates suitable for professional communication learning? Are statistical and specialty terms appropriately scaffolded for teenagers and adults? |
| Role boundaries | Does every expression remain within communication rather than authorizing care? Are professional titles, responsibilities and regional variants appropriate? |
| Hospital, clinic and departments | Are the facility, service and department distinctions appropriate across the intended regions? |
| Examination instruments | Verify otoscope, ophthalmoscope, dermatoscope, reflex hammer, spirometer and peak flow meter definitions and future artwork. No use technique is taught. |
| ECG and imaging distinctions | Keep the ECG machine separate from the electrocardiogram recording. Distinguish CT X-rays, MRI fields/radio waves, ultrasound sound and an X-ray image. |
| Body structures | Review every reused anatomical definition and any future illustration, including vessel direction, tissues, organs and body regions. |
| Body systems | Check broad system definitions, endocrine tissues, immune processes and overlaps without implying that systems are isolated. |
| Airway and digestive passage | Review esophagus, trachea, airway and mucous membrane definitions and illustration briefs. |
| Clinical versus technical diagnose | The new health-condition action intentionally differs from the existing technical-problem sense. Verify that examples do not become diagnostic algorithms. |
| Assessment language | Review elicit, synthesize, correlate, differentiate, exclude and suspect. Correlation must not imply causation; excluding a possibility requires contextual clinical judgment. |
| Clinical course | Check acute, chronic, intermittent, persistent, recurrent and progressive. The terms are not necessarily mutually exclusive or universal time thresholds. |
| Anatomical direction | Check bilateral/unilateral, proximal/distal and superficial/deep with explicit reference structures. |
| Condition descriptions | Benign is scoped to a noncancerous tumor context and does not promise harmlessness. Stable must state the respect and period, without implying normal health. |
| Congenital and acquired | Distinguish presence at birth from later development without equating acquired with transmissible or congenital with inherited. |
| History domains | Review family, social, surgical, occupational, travel, vaccination and smoking histories without stereotyping or unsupported assumptions. |
| Symptom language | Keep pain and symptoms based on the person's account; preserve distinctions between observed signs and reported experience. Radiation is pain spread here. |
| Medicines in history | Over-the-counter availability is jurisdiction-dependent. Herbal remedy conveys reported use, not effectiveness or safety. No dose or treatment instruction appears. |
| Laboratory interpretation | Reference intervals retain population/method context. A result or category alone must not establish diagnosis or health. |
| Sensitivity and specificity | Check the condition-based denominators and stated test conditions, avoiding confusion with predictive value. |
| False results and predictive value | Verify reference-standard language, condition status and population context. No frequency or universal accuracy claim is made. |
| Prevalence and likelihood ratio | Check statistical definitions, population/time scope and the probability ratio. Do not confuse likelihood ratio with a probability of disease. |
| Diagnostic yield | Confirm the stated defined outcome and setting; yield is not interchangeable with accuracy or sensitivity. |
| Diagnostic reasoning | Working diagnosis is provisional; differential diagnosis and diagnostic uncertainty preserve alternatives. Incidental findings are separate from the original question. |
| Screening and confirmatory tests | Check purpose and population wording, preserving that screening does not itself confirm a diagnosis and further testing does not guarantee certainty. |
| Consent and capacity | Keep decision/time specificity and applicable jurisdictional rules. A consent form alone does not establish valid agreement. A person's changed decision must be represented respectfully. |
| Shared decisions | Decision aids support dialogue; benefit-risk balance, burden and quality of life must reflect the person's priorities. No universally correct choice is implied. |
| Safety-netting | Review the regional term and its definition. Examples document agreed individualized arrangements without prescribing symptom thresholds or contact rules. |
| Adherence | Preserve an agreed-recommendation and nonjudgmental framing. Consider accessibility, practical barriers and treatment burden. |
| Infection and safety | Reused infection, inflammation, allergy, anaphylaxis, contraindication, asepsis, precautions and PPE require clinical review. No universal equipment selection or procedure is taught. |
| Records and digital support | Diagnostic codes do not establish diagnoses. Decision support does not replace judgment. Acknowledgment means receipt, not clinical acceptance or completion. |
| Care continuity | Check referral, triage, admission, discharge, transfer, rehabilitation and supervision against local responsibilities and arrangements. |
| Specialist roles | Review all specialist definitions. Anesthesiologist is not universally equivalent to anesthetist; pediatric age ranges and specialist titles vary. |
| Inclusive communication | Review preferences, interpreters, privacy, gender-related anatomy wording, mental-health language and nonjudgmental histories. |
| Word parts | All graphs remain pending. Review the three combining-form candidates and the endo- prefix plus -scopy suffix candidate. X-ray remains null because X is not an abbreviation; specialist titles and uncertain complex terms remain null. |
| Media | Future sourced professional artwork needs provenance, creator, license, acquisition status, suitability and animation information. Anatomical diagrams need medical verification. No appearance should imply diagnosis, competence or consent. |

## Consulted sources and limits

The consultation record is preserved in the scratch `sources.cjs` and `research-notes.md`, with nine entries:

1. [BLS Physicians and Surgeons](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/physicians-and-surgeons.htm): occupational duties, settings, specialist descriptions and communication.
2. [GMC patient communication](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/good-medical-practice/domain-2-patients-partnership-and-communication): UK patient partnership and communication context.
3. [GMC consent principles](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/decision-making-and-consent/the-seven-principles-of-decision-making-and-consent): the seven short principles, without universal legal assumptions.
4. [MedlinePlus laboratory results](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/how-to-understand-your-lab-results/): result categories, clinical context, reference ranges and limitations; no linked test articles.
5. [MedlinePlus diagnostic imaging](https://medlineplus.gov/diagnosticimaging.html): overview only; no linked modality procedures.
6. [GMC colleagues, culture and safety](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/good-medical-practice/domain-3-colleagues-culture-and-safety): continuity, delegation, records and reporting context.
7. [AHRQ diagnosis education](https://www.ahrq.gov/diagnostic-safety/resources/issue-briefs/education-dx-outcomes-2.html): this section only; no linked studies or full diagnostic curriculum.
8. [NHS consent overview](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/consent-to-treatment/): voluntary informed discussion and withdrawal context. The displayed review is from 2022, with an overdue 2025 review; exception and life-support passages are not used as current rules.
9. [MedlinePlus word parts](https://medlineplus.gov/appendixa.html): consulted parts table, not complete-word morphology approval.

All source references remain pending term checks. The inaccessible NICE recommendations page is excluded. No source asset licensing or acquisition is claimed.
