# Office Administration expert-review brief

## Scope and status

The Office Administration course contains a complete **draft inventory of 500 distinct canonical senses and 100 expressions**. The shared foundation contributes 100 senses and 20 expressions. Modules03 through module10 add 400 specialist memberships and 80 expressions: 178 new senses and 222 reused canonical senses. All modules remain draft. Every specialist entry and expression requires recorded domain review in addition to language review, including content outside module08. A `not_required` label cannot bypass this gate.

The content supports office communication, document recognition, coordination, and requests for current information. It provides no record-disposal deadline, universal retention rule, contract interpretation, entitlement to leave or reimbursement, tax calculation, cash-handling procedure, electrical setup instruction, equipment repair, or emergency technique. Naming an activity does not authorize a learner to carry it out.

All 178 new sense-specific picture briefs retain picture suitability false and pending media status. Word and expression recordings retain the approved Ava preset and remain pending. There are 137 explicit pending morphology graphs and 41 unauthored analyses. All new senses have an authored scored route through a relevant expression or a multi-part graph. This coverage does not make their unreviewed exercises playable.

## Review assignments

|---|---|---|
| Administrative educator, equipment specialist, and regional language reviewer | module03; copier, shredder, stapler, staple, paper clip, binder clip, hole punch, ring binder, letter opener, toner, printer cartridge, extension lead | Confirm equipment identity, ordinary usage, compatible-supply terminology, and regional alternatives. Copier/photocopier and extension lead/extension cord are accepted forms, not extra senses. Static equipment illustrations must not imply operating authority, repair guidance, safe connection arrangements, or handling of exposed blades or toner. Check the distinction between a stapler and its staple. |
| Business-writing educator and records specialist | module04; business letter, memo, salutation, subject line, correspondence, distribution list, mailing list, document, report, meeting minutes, transcript, executive summary, original document | Check natural definitions, distinctions, and workplace relevance. Document and report have new noun senses distinct from the existing verbs. Meeting minutes summarize relevant business and decisions; a transcript represents spoken content. A final version is final for an identified purpose or stage, not automatically approved for every use. A draft or final watermark is not itself proof of approval. |
| Qualified records and legal-domain reviewers | module04 and relevant expressions in modules05/08/10; contract, certified copy, visitor register, sign-in sheet, filing system, retention schedule, document register, archive, records manager | Confirm jurisdiction, record category, applicable holds, authority, and local terminology. A certified copy needs the receiving body's actual requirements and authorized process. A retention schedule does not permit automatic disposal in every situation. NARA guidance concerns U.S. federal records and must not become a universal office rule. Do not use real signatures, seals, personal details, retention periods, or disposal instructions in teaching assets. |
| Postal-services and procurement reviewers | registered mail, delivery confirmation, quotation, invoice, purchase requisition, purchase order, purchase approval; modules04/07 | Registered-mail features vary by provider and country; a recorded delivery event does not prove that a named person read the contents. Quotation, invoice, internal request, purchase order, approval, and delivery record are distinct stages or documents. A vocabulary entry cannot establish contractual acceptance, debt validity, or authority to spend. Verify invoice versus the broader shared bill sense before sign-off. |
| Office-process educator | module05; type, proofread, edit, format, print, photocopy, collate, file, retrieve, archive, circulate, distribute, dispatch, transcribe, dictate, minute, book, delegate | Verify usage and scope. Minute is a verb about meeting records, not a short duration. Book means arrange a reservation, not a reading object. Archiving is managed storage, not destruction or an automatic retention decision. Delegation and assignment must stay within actual role authority. Confirm that the reused document verb is suitable in nonclinical records examples. |
| Meeting secretary, accessibility specialist, and travel coordinator | module06; attendance list, apology for absence, quorum, chairperson, minute taker, itinerary, accommodation, connection time, hybrid meeting, scheduling poll | Verify local meeting conventions and travel vocabulary. Quorum depends on the body's rules and can concern specific business; do not invent a minimum number. Expected and actual attendance are different. Travel approval is not a guarantee of cost coverage or a successful connection. Check current provider information and time zones, including changes across recurring meetings. Ask about individual access needs and provide an alternative response route when a scheduling poll is inaccessible. Screen sharing alone may not provide accessible materials. |
| Finance, expense-policy, and procurement reviewers | module07; budget, cost center, expense claim, reimbursement, petty cash, petty cash voucher, expense category, invoice number, due date, payment approval | Confirm classifications, process boundaries, and regional terms. An expense claim is not a promise of reimbursement. Purchasing authorization is separate from payment approval, ordering, and receipt. Petty cash remains a concept and record vocabulary; the lesson does not prescribe handling steps or limits. Tax and currency words do not authorize a calculation or financial decision. All illustrated amounts and identifiers must be fictional. |
| Privacy, security, facilities, and workplace-safety reviewers | module08; visitor badge, access card, clear desk policy, screen lock, ergonomics, data minimization, retention period, authorized representative | Distinguish identity from authority and report missing credentials through the approved route. Preserve the prohibition on posting passwords or one-time codes in messages. A screen lock is one control, not a complete security guarantee. Clear-desk arrangements protect records rather than authorizing disposal. Ergonomics should fit the individual; no single posture or layout is prescribed. Physical safety examples report concerns and refer to the actual local process. |
| Office-software educator, accessibility reviewer, and data-control reviewer | module09; download, upload, formula, cell reference, worksheet, workbook, font, heading, footer, spellcheck, mail merge, track changes, comment thread, read-only access | Verify common meanings across software products. A worksheet is a sheet within a workbook. A formula's result still needs the correct inputs and references. Spellcheck cannot establish semantic accuracy. Mail merge needs actual recipient and output checks; review permissions and personal-data handling. Tracked changes and comments may expose information if shared incorrectly. Read-only access does not imply that copying or further distribution is permitted. Use original fictional interfaces rather than copied vendor screens. |
| Administrative workforce, HR, and regional role reviewers | module10; administrative assistant, office clerk, office manager, executive assistant, facilities coordinator, records manager, procurement officer, job description, induction, leave request | Confirm role names and actual boundaries. O*NET's general administrative-assistant classification excludes legal, medical, and executive specialties; recognition of a title is not a combined qualification. A role title does not establish approval authority. Induction is workplace orientation, not a guarantee of complete training. Leave requests and staffing changes require the applicable decision process; the examples do not state an entitlement. |
| English morphologist | all 137 graphs and 41 null analyses; copier, shredder, stapler, certified copy, incoming mail, proofread, photocopy, out-of-office message, read-only access | Verify true constituents, semantic transparency, allomorphs, lexicalization, and deeper analysis of intact stems. Copier preserves copy with the surface copi before er; shredder preserves the doubled consonant; photocopy explicitly identifies a combining form. Multiword terms preserve spaces and hyphens. Cupboard and other lexicalized compounds need semantic review. Null does not mean atomic, and occupational pages are not morphology evidence. |
| Art director, language educator, and accessibility reviewer | all 178 new picture briefs | Acquire and inspect production artwork. Keep office scenes inclusive, friendly, and free from sensitive details, stereotypes, official-looking credentials, real seals, or equipment-use demonstrations. Distinguish stationery meanings from digital controls. Abstract records and actions may need context scenes and must not silently enter picture-identification practice. Record provenance, visual review at gameplay size, and actual exercise suitability independently. |
