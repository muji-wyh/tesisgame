# Jelly Words powerups

Auto merge and goal-family purchases share a quiet pearl surface.
Wide controls place the sourced artwork beside a left-aligned name and coin-price
column, borrowing the pending word goals' reading order. Narrow phone tiles keep
a full-width title above the artwork and price, without squeezing the name beside
the icon. Actual four-digit prices retain readable lettering. Neutral
cream surfaces share one border treatment; the sourced artwork identifies the
tool instead of giving every card a different colored frame.
Their artwork stays separate from the stable touch target, title, and coin price.
Auto merge costs 200, 300, then 400 coins per round at owned Lv1.
Each owned level adds 50 coins to the first-use base through 600 at Lv9;
repeat use multiplies that base by 1.5, then 2.
Goal-family merges cost 50, 75, then 100 per net board space freed.
Each item tracks successful purchases separately
and shows its next price without resetting the current feedback animation.
Treasure and the paid +30 sec card are retired. Neither is constructed in the
live action rail, advertised in its price snapshot, or accepted by the model or
application wallet adapter. Natural chest fragments, earned treasure and
Challenge goal-time awards remain intact. Historical quote values and already
committed delivery completion do not enable new purchases.

The purchase controls, Fill and Collect share one action group. The compact
icon-only Fill control sits beside the wider Collect action wherever both fit;
only a narrow rail places it above Collect. Auto merge is the only separate paid
tool, below the free controls in a side rail or footer. Retired cards reserve no
empty rows or columns, and the remaining group uses the reclaimed space.
Ordinary paid controls are
56 display pixels high, using compact 44-pixel controls on short screens and Boss
layouts. Fill always keeps a 44-pixel target. The group retains bottom padding.
Their combined bounds span the same width as the paid tools below.
Fill is one 56-pixel-wide whole-control switch, not a long capsule or a button
containing a smaller toggle. Its acquired gel illustration stays centered:
clear blue cells are still when Off; On changes the entire surface to mint and
feeds a larger green cell downward into the two source-art cells below.
The acquired downward chevron belongs beside this supply illustration rather
than at the far edge like a dropdown. One light border also carries focus,
without drawing another ring around the same control. The cue stays alive while
the mode is On, including when the board is full or Challenge locks the switch.
The existing lock replaces the chevron in Challenge while the supply stays active. There are no
Fill, On, Off, tooltip, or helper labels. The complete name and selected/required
state remain accessible to screen readers.
One quiet cream backing groups the controls; only Collect is a strong green
primary action. The divider remains visible but uses the same fine neutral edge.

Hovering or focusing Auto Collect previews the batch's actual captured Jelly
IDs with the existing matching halo and dims the other board bodies. Completed
Jellies still landing and maximum-level Jellies remain eligible; unfinished,
held, fusing, frozen and locked bodies do not. The existing animation budget
limits moving highlights while every eligible body retains a visible halo.
Leaving, disabling or activating the button clears the preview, as do pause,
drag and view cancellation. Collection blasts remain separate downstream effects.

## Permanent Auto merge capacity

The existing **Jelly Upgrades** page has separate **Merged Jelly** and **Auto
merge** category controls. Auto merge starts at free Lv1 with up to three completed,
collectable Jellies per use. Each next level adds one output, reaching eleven at Lv9.
The independent permanent upgrades cost 50, 100, 200, 350, 550, 800, 1100 and
1500 coins. The owned level also determines its per-use price base; repeat
purchase counters reset each round, while permanent ownership remains saved.
The existing v4 Jelly ownership and pending receipts migrate without repricing;
v5 records both levels and the pending upgrade kind. A paid final-save retry
always follows that kind, even after switching category.

The button retains its acquired Kenney wand and coin-price column. A quiet
violet badge pairs the existing acquired mint gel with the real capacity number;
the most compact tiles attach the same number to the wand without squeezing
the full title or coin price. The badge stays stable during pause, reduced
motion, purchase feedback and price escalation. It is an owned per-use capacity,
not the number of matches left in the current animation. The accessible name
includes the saved level, capacity and real use price.
The category preview reuses those same static acquired materials to show
exactly three through eleven illustrated Jellies; it is a capacity illustration,
not a recorded merge performance or newly acquired animation.

Only complete legal paths are reserved, one per output slot. Original ingredients
and intermediate joins do not consume these slots. Each finished product is
excluded from further matching during the same use, so three advertised results
cannot collapse into one upgraded Jelly. Unmatched ingredients and missing pictures
are left intact; fewer available paths yield fewer complete results, never invented
partners or a paid partial-only spelling. Already reserved paths finish on pause/exit through the existing
transaction lifecycle. Goal-family and collection-blast matching are unchanged.

The accepted receipt starts a 100 ms hit-stop before any drag is launched. Existing
world-motion and presentation clocks share that hold, including airborne supply
and Boss animation. The acquired portal glow, rays and glints add one finite
violet impact and recovery at the well, not a dark overlay or repeated flash.
The existing 320 ms flights then launch every 100 ms. Pausing or hiding preserves
the same receipt clock; reduced motion keeps a soft still light without traveling
rays or particles. A failed payment produces no successful impact. No new sound
asset or additional voice is introduced.

Hovering or keyboard/controller-focusing Auto merge previews the exact original
Jellies its next use will consume, within the owned completed-output capacity.
It is not a highlight of every matching word family or only the first pair:
multi-step spellings include all required roots and pictures. Already airborne,
captured bodies are included when the real timeline would use them after landing.
The model forecasts with its existing automatic timeline on isolated board data,
without payment callbacks, shared random generators or a live-world pause.
Unchanged board forecasts are cached; relevant motion, statuses and material
changes refresh the targets.

The preview reuses the acquired gel skins' existing drag-match silhouette halo,
gentle growth and subdued competing material light. It adds no artwork, arrows,
screen cover or hover pronunciation. Reduced motion keeps the same target IDs
and still material contrast, without growth. Leaving the button, changing focus,
pause, dragging and navigation clear it. An activation consumes the preview;
pointer activation also releases acquired button focus, so completion cannot
leave an eternal selected state. A fresh hover or navigation focus previews the
new board. Prices, capacity, impact, flight timing and paid delivery are unchanged.

## Goal-family auto merge

The standalone Magnet button and its second target-selection gesture are removed.
Each pending word goal is now the purchase target. Its acquired word illustration
and full name occupy the first reading line; the real coin price and completion
count have separate places below. The narrow left column keeps both values instead
of hiding the count. Hovering or focusing a row gives it a mint edge and lightly
lifts its illustration. Corresponding board Jellies use the existing drag-match
silhouette halo, gel growth and subdued competing material light. Hover is free;
leaving, pause, dragging and navigation clear it without moving hit geometry.
Activating a goal ends that hover/focus preview. Mouse and touch activation also
release their acquired button focus, so finishing or rejecting the purchase
returns the card and board to normal even if the pointer has not moved.
Leaving and re-entering the card, or focusing it again through navigation,
starts a fresh preview. Keyboard/controller focus retains its ordinary focus
indicator without latching the consumed family preview.
Clicking or activating that goal uses its exact word identity directly. No random
target is substituted, and no coins are charged unless this family's current
board inputs have a complete ordinary merge path.
Roots, pictures and different merged levels are grouped by their actual word
identity, never their gel color or printed substring.

One saved purchase uses the existing overlapping automatic-flight planner.
It preserves unlocked levels, real occupancy, carried fragments and frozen/locked
eligibility. Only legal complete paths are consumed; missing partners are not
invented. Other families stay on the board. It never automatically collects,
grants construction mastery or mints manual-merge combo coins. A later successful
collection owns its usual learning and rewards. All goal rows share the existing
per-round family-use price tier, independent of Auto merge; the price does not
reset for a different goal.
Each goal multiplies this unit tier by the net board reduction forecast for its
full legal family timeline. Combining four existing bodies into one frees three
spaces and costs 150 coins at the first tier. Unmatched roots, capped Jellies,
frozen or locked bodies and other untouched leftovers do not inflate the quote.
Intermediate joins are accounted for through the net reduction, without double
counting. The selected quote is captured before the wallet call; display,
affordability, saved debit and shortage feedback use that same exact amount.

A newly saved payment reuses the existing wallet debit: rolling digits, an exact
negative amount, and one acquired gold coin traveling from the wallet to the
clicked goal's price. That row briefly shows the actual amount spent while its
price already advertises the next use. Local cost glow and coin motion belong to
the accepted receipt, not to a learning-success event. Rejected, duplicate,
unavailable and completed-goal actions cannot animate a debit or advance prices.
Reduced motion retains the exact amount and local still light without flight.
The request stays bound to its original goal before input cancellation and
payment callbacks; a nested press cannot replace that row, quote or debit cue.

Fill continuously releases real advertised supply into each physically clear
entrance. Later Jellies begin falling while earlier ones are still airborne,
without waiting for a whole packet to land or settle. Landing space is reserved
before departure, and entrance spacing follows the real fall speed so bodies
cannot pass through one another. Preview replacements wait for that same real
entrance clearance, including faster stages, rather than fading over a body
that is still leaving the tray. It stops when no reachable vacancy remains;
switching it off leaves already dispatched Jellies to finish falling normally.
It never fills instantly or bypasses paid-operation, drag, pause, save or ending
ownership. Main/unbound play has an independent optional preference.
Challenge always enables Fill and disables its
switch; this forced value never overwrites the ordinary preference.

Items remain usable while unrelated supply is falling. Auto merge and goal-family merges
still need a legal settled pair; incoming supply is not mistaken for unsettled
board support that would make an operation unsafe. A saved item temporarily owns
new supply, while already dispatched Jellies continue their normal descent.
After the item finishes, Fill resumes automatically without changing its selected
preference. Retired Treasure and extra-time requests cannot charge, add fragments
or extend a clock, including while Fill is active.

## Feedback and lifecycle

Successful purchases give the sourced icon a short upward pop, a restrained
turn, and local glints over 0.62 seconds. Rejection uses a 0.42-second damped
sideways recoil and warm amber light. Titles and prices stay level, and the
touch target never changes. An insufficient balance uses a muted price row
and warm readable price text while preserving clickability; only unavailable or busy actions
are disabled. There are no tooltips or explanatory overlays.

Insufficient coins have a more explicit, separate 1.15-second response. The
clicked card briefly says **Need coins**, warms its surface and rim, and makes
the acquired coin and unchanged price row recoil and settle. The tool artwork
stays still: the cost, not a broken wand or chest, is the cause. The existing
`gold-coin.png` and sourced local glow are reused without new artwork or
celebration sparkles. The accessible name retains the actual tool, exact quoted
cost and actual missing-coin amount. The ordinary title returns automatically.
Rapid retries restart one bounded response; they do not stack effects, debit
coins, advance item prices, open a dialog or move the hit area.

The wallet's explicit `reason: insufficient` outcome owns this explanation.
A failed save, duplicate receipt or unavailable goal path keeps its own normal
rejection and cannot inherit an old low-balance hint. A goal click receives the
same authoritative shortfall as any other purchase, without a free selector
step or an unrelated-family fallback. Unknown wallet balances are not treated as known zero coins.
Pause hides and freezes the transient warning; reduced motion keeps its readable
warm state and wording without coin scaling or price motion, including when the
preference changes mid-response.

Auto merge retains its exclusive board operation. An already committed legacy
delivery can still finish safely; retiring its card neither refunds nor changes
old immutable wallet receipts.

Collect remains the wide green primary action beside compact Fill, with a sourced word-jelly illustration,
a stable title and a contained completed-word count badge. During collection
the number becomes the remaining count; the full meaning stays available in
the existing accessible name. Narrow controls retain the same bounded number
under the title. No coin illustration suggests a collection fee. The mint, sky and
lilac gel skins and Microsoft Fluent Emoji cat are existing integrated artwork;
their unchanged source, creator, license and acquisition records are linked in
[Jelly Words](jelly-words.md). The empty state keeps the illustration and zero count visible. Typography fits
both width and line height in the existing UI scale, without the old eight-unit
minimum that overflowed highly scaled desktop controls. Accumulated words retain the existing charge, growth,
local light and sparkle rhythm, while each pickup gives the illustration a
small compress-and-lift beat. This styling does not change collection
eligibility or reward amounts, and count changes never move the hit area.

The game owner advances the button clock. Pausing freezes the clock and hides
the transient pose and light; hidden controls do not advance. Reset clears the
transient presentation without issuing a purchase. Reduced motion uses a static
0.32-second light accent with no scale, translation, or rotation. The minimum
target is 88 by 56 display pixels normally, or 88 by 44 in compact layouts,
scaled through the existing UI scale.

Fill has its own persistent mode clock rather than borrowing a purchase's
short success effect. The downward cycle lasts 1.4 seconds, with a quiet
2.8-second local glow breath; the whole surface changes tint over 160 ms. These effects
do not dispatch supply or modify a gameplay clock. Pause freezes the pose and
retains the active style. Reduced motion retains the mint surface, downward
icon and local glow without continuous motion. Hidden/finished
controls stop advancing, and reset clears only presentation. On/Off, Challenge
ownership and the ordinary preference are unchanged. The compact target stays
at least 56 by 44 display pixels; its illustration never moves sideways between
states. Forced, paused and reduced-motion states settle the surface to the
actual selected state immediately.

Paid controls use the normal Button `pressed` signal; Fill retains its native
`toggled` path. The model and wallet own eligibility, spending, rewards, busy
state, and cancellation; button animation never commits a transaction.

## Reused sound and world effects

The game owner plays the existing pair-feedback `right.wav` on a committed
purchase and `wrong.wav` for insufficient funds, both at the established 0.48
gain. These are reused from `assets/imported-audio/pair-feedback/`; their existing
user-supplied provenance and unspecified source license remain unchanged. No
new extraction, source audio claim, or CC0 attribution is applied to them.
The source recording, processing and embedded-game limits remain documented in
[answer feedback sounds](pair-feedback-audio.md).

Auto merge and goal-family merges select an eligible pair on each 100 ms launch beat, starting
immediately after purchase. Each automatic flight lasts 320 ms, followed by a
110 ms fusion and 30 ms settlement. Consecutive flights overlap for 220 ms;
the next launch does not wait for the preceding 460 ms sequence to finish.
Manual gestures retain their existing timing. Every moving source and its
target stay reserved until contact, and each path preserves the inputs needed
by its later steps. Independent pairs can launch while earlier paths continue.
A step that needs an earlier result waits for that result to settle. If no pair
is eligible, the operation waits without compressing missed beats. The existing
arcing motion, gentle stretch, target glow, and sparkle trail follow each flight's
own clock. Contact starts the liquid merge and its cue at the destination.
Completed words remain
on the board until the player collects them.

Automatic movement reuses the ordinary pick, contact, and merge cues. Archived
Treasure flights used the existing chest texture, `portal_glow.png`, and
`milestone/sparkle.png`. Each scheduled fragment launch plays
`assets/audio/fragments/appear.wav`, and its delivery plays
`assets/audio/fragments/arrive.wav`, both at gain 0.45. These distinct acquired
Kenney CC0 recordings, their preparation and their independent four-channel
mix are documented in [fragment audio](jelly-fragments.md#fragment-audio).
The legacy first flight arrives after 0.42 seconds and subsequent deliveries are
staggered by 0.18 seconds. Launches and arrivals each emit once at their actual
timeline boundaries, including overlapping purchases, and do not replay after
a pause. The cues belong to the owner's committed purchase and delivery
events; the button itself plays no sound.
