## Required qualified review

Language and assessment reviewers should confirm that each expression actually assesses every listed sense, reads naturally in the stated setting, and suits its provisional difficulty. A literal word match alone is not evidence of semantic assessment. Check long lists and compound focus terms for learner load, especially expressions with multiple utensils, ingredients, handling actions, or system controls. Keep repeated occurrences and punctuation intact if wording changes.

Occupational reviewers should check these boundaries against the actual workplace and region:

- Caregiving and Nursing: recognition and reporting language, consent, role authority, medicine terminology, safeguarding, and infection concerns. No diagnosis, dosage, treatment choice, or procedure is prescribed.
- Retail, Food Service, and Hospitality: payment and refund conditions, optional tips, distinct date labels, allergens, sanitation terminology, access needs, restricted sales, and task-specific protective equipment. Requests for guidance must not become universal safety or eligibility rules.
- Customer Support, Office Administration, and Warehouse: account authority, privacy, incident records, handling arrangements, workflow responsibility, and the actual organization's policy scope.
- Software & Data: computational senses, data structures, statistical terms, service commitments, and the distinction between technical and everyday meanings.

Specific editorial distinctions include the same-product meaning of variant; explicit noun and verb usages; the processing queue of booking requests rather than a queue of people; a standalone stock, menu, course, desk, chef, or cook focus rather than accidental substring coverage; and exact verbs such as unload, stir, whisk, blend, and launder.

## Presentation and media review
